A five-minute routine

The page follows Read → Answer → Check. Each challenge now asks one question. Students move through three short, headed extracts, use a relevant picture and open word help when needed. Tapping a useful sentence saves it as a clue; they still explain the answer in their own words. A direct link below the reading opens the original lesson section.

  1. Set the job — 30 seconds. Open one challenge or spin the wheel within the current module. Read the prompt aloud. Students can type, write in their books or prepare a spoken response.
  2. Read and find — two minutes. Students read the three short parts and select a useful sentence. Ask why that sentence helps answer the question. Allow more time where needed.
  3. Pair and repair — one minute. Partners compare answers and point to their evidence. Each student improves one part of their explanation. A student working alone can reread and improve their own answer.
  4. Hear the reasoning — one minute. Invite two different answers. Ask, “Which part of the text helped you decide?” Use the look-for guidance to check the reasoning together.
  5. Close the loop — 30 seconds. Students finish: “I now understand … because …”. Decide whether the class is ready for the next lesson activity.

Three useful choices

Text huntUse when students need to notice key ideas or relationships. They locate the evidence, then explain what it means. The wheel selects a challenge; you can also choose directly.
Fix the dodgy answerUse when a familiar misconception could hide behind a fluent answer. Students identify what is misleading and rewrite it using the theory. Every false claim is explicitly labelled as a claim to challenge.
Make a decisionUse when students should transfer the reading to a small project situation. Ask for a decision and the evidence behind it. Workshop decisions always remain subject to current teacher instruction and authorisation.

Choose one routine when it helps. These challenges are alternatives for teaching the current section, not a new compulsory worksheet sequence. The 25-period program, theory order, existing knowledge checks, folio and assessment requirements retain their current roles.

Keep preparation and marking small

Project the challenge and use books or talk if devices are unavailable. Each full challenge includes the source reading and a printable response sheet. There is no cutting out, account creation or teacher setup. Use the wheel within the current module so the class is practising material you are teaching.

Digital responses save in this browser and can be revisited from the lesson or challenge bank. Students reveal the look-for guidance after attempting both fields, then record their own review. A reviewed response means the student checked it; it does not claim that their answer is correct or teacher marked. The course-home backup includes these optional records.

Source and program relationship

The nineteen challenges use the existing fifteen Desk Tidy theory sections and their authorised project source boundary. Weeks 1–2 has seven wheel choices, including four extra questions about criteria, safety controls, housekeeping and material waste. The fifteen original challenges remain beside their lesson sections; the extra choices are in the module wheel and bank. They add no dimensions, equipment permissions, practical requirements, assessment rules or outcome claims. This guide is a teaching-method supplement to the existing 25-period teaching program, with each challenge linked to its original section.

The course's current syllabus remains the NESA Technology 7–8 Syllabus (2023), with implementation from 2026. Its overview includes creating written texts to develop and communicate understanding. The existing project unit and current teacher directions continue to control practical work.
